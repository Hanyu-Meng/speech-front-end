THE ONE MENTAL MODEL

Every task is structured signal recovery

Start from what is observed, what side information exists, and what must be preserved.

01

Define the target

Clean speech, one enrolled speaker, anechoic speech, or near-end speech?

02

List the evidence

Mixture only, far-end reference, multiple microphones, enrollment audio, DOA, HRTF, vision, or text?

03

Choose the estimator

Signal-processing filter, discriminative mapping, generative prior, or a hybrid cascade?

04

Protect invariants

Words, speaker identity, spatial cues, direct path, near-end speech, latency, and causality.

FOUNDATIONS

The concepts every architecture assumes

Master these first. Model names become much easier once you can place them on these axes.

Waveform vs. STFT

Waveform models avoid explicit phase handling. STFT models expose magnitude, phase, and spatial covariance, making filtering and beamforming easier to express.

Masking vs. mapping

A mask scales mixture coefficients and keeps a strong observation anchor. Direct mapping predicts the target representation and is more flexible but less constrained.

Causal vs. noncausal

Causal systems use past and limited look-ahead. Noncausal systems can use full utterances. Always ask for algorithmic latency, RTF, memory, and target hardware.

Single vs. multichannel

Single-channel systems rely on spectral and learned priors. Multiple microphones add spatial covariance, phase differences, beamforming, and geometry mismatch.

Conditioning

TSE and AEC are defined by side information: speaker embedding, spatial cue, far-end reference, or multimodal prompt. Condition quality sets the performance ceiling.

Loss vs. metric

Training losses must be differentiable; evaluation metrics need not be. Optimizing a proxy can improve its score while harming words, identity, or naturalness.

Closed-set vs. open-world

Benchmarks control speakers, rooms, arrays, and distortions. Deployment adds unseen devices, sample rates, languages, motion, echo paths, and nonlinearities.

Physical constraint vs. learned prior

WPE, FCP, LAEC, and beamforming encode acoustic structure. Neural priors model speech. Strong systems often combine both instead of forcing one to do everything.

Speech Enhancement

Browse SE papers

SE estimates cleaner speech from a degraded observation. The real objective is not “remove everything that is not speech”; it is “remove unwanted distortion without changing the words, speaker, timing, or spatial intent.”

Observation
y = h ∗ s + n + d

h is the acoustic path, n is additive noise, and d covers clipping, bandwidth loss, codec artifacts, or other distortions.

Output contract

Preserve linguistic content and speaker identity; improve quality and intelligibility; respect latency, sample rate, and channel constraints.

01

Method map

Know what each family predicts and what anchors it to the observation.

Physical

Statistical filtering and beamforming

Spectral subtraction, Wiener/MMSE estimators, MVDR, and multichannel filters use noise or spatial statistics. They are efficient and interpretable but depend on accurate statistics and stationarity.

Discriminative

Masking, complex mapping, and direct waveform estimation

CRN/UNet, TasNet, TF-GridNet, MP-SENet, SpatialNet, and Mamba variants predict masks, magnitude/phase, complex spectra, filters, or waveforms in one forward pass.

Generative

Diffusion, bridges, flow, and speech language models

SGMSE+ uses score-based reverse dynamics; SE-Bridge and SB-RF shorten the transport path; SpeechFlow and FlowSE learn continuous flows; SELM and UniPASE operate through tokens or learned representations.

Hybrid

Predict first, regenerate carefully

StoRM, Extract-and-Diffuse, GAP-URGENet, and URGENT systems use a discriminative estimate or representation to anchor content before generative refinement or fusion.

Scenario changes

  • Single-channel: rely on spectral and speech priors.
  • Multichannel: add SCM estimation, beamforming, geometry, and array mismatch.
  • Binaural: preserve ILD/IPD/ITD and hearing-aid constraints.
  • Streaming: require causal state, bounded look-ahead, stable long-form behavior, and device-level RTF.
  • Universal: handle variable sample rates and multiple simultaneous distortions.

Failure modes to name

  • Speech deletion at low SNR or during nonstationary noise.
  • Musical noise, phase artifacts, or over-smoothed consonants.
  • Generative word hallucination and speaker/timbre drift.
  • Objective-score gains without human preference or ASR gains.
  • Offline quality that collapses under causal deployment.

Why can a generative enhancer sound better but be less trustworthy?

A strong speech prior can replace uncertain evidence with plausible speech. That may raise naturalness scores while changing phonemes, speaker traits, or timing. Pair perceptual metrics with WER, speaker similarity, signal fidelity, and listening tests.

Masking or mapping—which is better?

Masking is observation-anchored and often stable; mapping is less constrained and can reconstruct missing components. The right choice depends on degradation severity, phase treatment, and whether hallucination risk is acceptable.

What makes a real-time claim convincing?

Look for causality, explicit look-ahead, algorithmic latency, RTF on named hardware, memory growth on long audio, and stable streaming evaluation—not only a small parameter count.

Target Speaker Extraction

Browse TSE papers

TSE is conditional separation: recover one target speaker, not merely any dominant speech. The central research question is how the system knows whom to extract and how it behaves when that evidence is unreliable.

Observation
y = starget + ∑jsj + n

The model also receives a clue c: enrollment speech, speaker embedding, DOA, HRTF, visual motion, text, or a combination.

Output contract

Select the correct identity; suppress competing speakers and noise; avoid leakage when the target is absent; preserve content, voice, and spatial cues.

02

Method map

Organize TSE by cue, fusion point, separator, and output representation.

Speaker cue

Embedding-conditioned masking or separation

SpeakerBeam-style systems encode enrollment speech and inject it through adaptation, attention, FiLM, or concatenation. Conv-TasNet and state-space variants support causal low-latency extraction.

Spatial cue

DOA, geometry, HRTF, and beamformer guidance

BG-TSE, iNeuBe-X, GC-SSF, and HRTF-conditioned models use direction or array evidence. They improve selection but inherit DOA, calibration, and geometry errors.

Multimodal

Visual, text, and composable clues

SEANet uses audio-visual attention; WeSep separates cue modules from the backbone so enrollment, spatial, visual, and textual evidence can be combined or ablated.

Generative

Conditional diffusion, flow, and codec LMs

Conditional Diffusion TSE and DiffSpEx sample a target distribution; AlphaFlowTSE targets one-step transport; StarTSE streams autoregressively; LauraTSE and Mask2Flow use discriminative front-ends to anchor speaker selection.

Cue questions

  • Enrollment: clean or noisy; short or long; same channel and language?
  • Spatial: known DOA or tracked; fixed geometry or flexible array?
  • Binaural: generic or listener-specific HRTF; static or moving?
  • Visual: synchronized face video; what happens under occlusion?
  • Fusion: early conditioning, layer-wise modulation, cross-attention, or output selection?

Failure modes to name

  • Wrong-speaker extraction or identity swapping during overlap.
  • Target leakage when the target is silent or absent.
  • Enrollment mismatch, contamination, or insufficient duration.
  • Loss of binaural localization cues after extraction.
  • Tracking lag for moving speakers and cross trajectories.

How is TSE different from blind speech separation?

Blind separation returns multiple anonymous sources and may suffer permutation ambiguity. TSE returns one requested source by conditioning on identity or location; its unique risks are clue mismatch, wrong-target selection, and target-absent leakage.

Why is SI-SDR alone insufficient for TSE?

It does not verify identity or target-absent behavior. Report speaker similarity or confusion rate, content error such as WER/TER, activity detection, leakage, and perceptual quality alongside SI-SDRi.

What changes for a moving speaker?

A static embedding may still identify the target, but spatial filters and DOA cues must update over time. Evaluate tracking delay, identity swaps at crossings, causal state stability, and performance across trajectory speed.

DR · 11 PAPERS IN THE CATALOG

Dereverberation

Browse DR papers

Dereverberation estimates speech with less late room response. It is not the same as denoising: reverberation is a convolution of the desired speech with a room impulse response, so the interference is correlated with the source.

Observation
y[t] = ∑kh[k]s[t−k] + n[t]

The direct path and early reflections may be useful; long late reflections smear phonemes and degrade ASR. The target definition must state what portion of the RIR remains.

Output contract

Reduce late reverberation while preserving direct-path speech, natural early energy, phase coherence, and spatial consistency.

03

Method map

Move from linear prediction to learned filters, direct mapping, and generative priors.

Physical

WPE and forward convolutive prediction

WPE predicts late reverberation from delayed past frames. FCP reconstructs microphone observations from an estimated source. Both encode room structure and benefit strongly from multiple channels.

Filtering

Deep filter estimation and correlation modeling

IF-CorrNet uses inter-frame STFT correlations to estimate multiframe filters. USDnet combines a neural source estimate with forward filtering to train without clean targets.

Mapping

Complex spectral and joint spatial-spectral networks

TF-GridNet, SpatialNet, DDNet, and DeepVQE learn dereverberation jointly with denoising or enhancement. Task-decoupled cascades reduce objective conflict but accumulate errors.

Generative

Score priors and stochastic regeneration

SGMSE+ jointly handles noise and reverberation; StoRM anchors diffusion with a predictive estimate; preference-aligned restoration improves perceived quality but still needs fidelity checks.

What changes by setup

  • Single-channel: inverse problem is highly ambiguous; learned speech priors dominate.
  • Multichannel: spatial diversity supports prediction and filter estimation.
  • Real rooms: RIR mismatch, moving sources, and microphone response matter.
  • Joint denoise + dereverb: order and loss balance change the error profile.
  • Unsupervised: physical reconstruction prevents arbitrary outputs but can admit degenerate solutions.

Failure modes to name

  • Removing direct-path or useful early-reflection energy.
  • Dry, metallic, or phase-incoherent output.
  • Simulated-RIR gains that do not transfer to real rooms.
  • SRMR improvement without ASR or listening improvement.
  • Failure under time-varying rooms and overlapping speakers.

Why is dereverberation harder than additive denoising?

The reverberant component is a delayed, filtered copy of the speech itself. It overlaps the target in time and frequency, so separating “clean” from “reverberant” energy requires a target definition and temporal/acoustic assumptions.

WPE, FCP, or direct neural mapping?

WPE explicitly predicts late tails; FCP uses a source estimate to explain observations; direct mapping learns the target without an explicit filter. Hybrids gain structure and flexibility but depend on both the neural estimate and filter assumptions.

How do you evaluate without a clean reference?

Use SRMR or learned non-intrusive quality estimators cautiously, plus downstream far-field ASR and human listening. Report room, distance, T60, DRR, and whether the system was trained on matched RIRs.

AEC · 8 PAPERS IN THE CATALOG

Acoustic Echo Cancellation

Browse AEC papers

AEC removes the loudspeaker echo of a known far-end reference from the microphone signal. Its hardest operating point is double-talk: near-end speech and echo are active together, so aggressive removal can destroy the desired speaker.

Observation
y = snear + hecho ∗ xfar + n

x_far is available. The challenge is estimating a time-varying and often nonlinear echo path while preserving s_near.

Output contract

Suppress echo during far-end single-talk; preserve near-end speech during double-talk; recover quickly after delay or echo-path changes; remain stable in real time.

04

Method map

AEC architectures differ by where explicit echo-path modeling ends and learned suppression begins.

Classical

Adaptive linear echo cancellation

NLMS, frequency-domain adaptive filters, and related LAEC methods estimate the linear echo path from the far-end reference. Double-talk detectors and control logic protect adaptation.

Hybrid

LAEC plus neural residual echo suppression

LMPAN and EchoLite align and fuse microphone, reference, and LAEC outputs before a compact post-filter. This preserves a physical anchor while learning nonlinear residuals and noise suppression.

End-to-end

Joint alignment and enhancement

DeepVQE uses cross-attention and recurrent modeling; E2E-AEC transfers LAEC knowledge and adds attention alignment and VAD control. End-to-end systems simplify the pipeline but hide failure causes.

Generative

Far-end-conditioned score or diffusion restoration

FADI-AEC designs a fast score process guided by the far-end signal; DiffVQE uses conditional diffusion for joint echo and noise restoration. Fidelity and causality remain the key concerns.

Stress conditions

  • Far-end single-talk: maximize echo attenuation without residual coloration.
  • Near-end single-talk: pass desired speech transparently.
  • Double-talk: suppress echo without near-end clipping or modulation.
  • Path change: reconverge rapidly after device or room movement.
  • Nonlinearity and delay: handle loudspeaker distortion, clock drift, and misalignment.

Failure modes to name

  • Near-end speech suppression during double-talk.
  • Residual echo after nonlinear loudspeaker distortion.
  • Divergence or slow reconvergence after path changes.
  • Reference/microphone time misalignment and long-delay drift.
  • Good ERLE with unpleasant near-end quality.
Catalog anchorsLMPAN · EchoLite · Personalized two-stage PFDeepVQE · E2E-AECFADI-AEC · DiffVQE
Why is the far-end reference so important?

It identifies the signal that created the echo. Classical filters estimate its transfer path; neural systems use it for alignment and conditional suppression. Without it, the problem becomes general separation or enhancement rather than standard AEC.

Why does high ERLE not prove good AEC?

ERLE mainly measures echo reduction where echo is present. A system can achieve high ERLE by suppressing near-end speech or over-attenuating. Pair it with double-talk near-end quality, AECMOS, WER, and scenario-stratified listening.

Hybrid or end-to-end?

Hybrid systems retain LAEC interpretability and convergence behavior while learning residual suppression. End-to-end models can jointly align and enhance but require broader training data and stronger diagnostics for delay, double-talk, and nonlinear failure.

ANSWER FRAMEWORK

A six-move answer for almost any question

Use this sequence in interviews, paper discussions, reviews, and research meetings.

  1. 1

    Define the task mathematically

    Name the observation, desired signal, interference, acoustic path, and side information.

  2. 2

    State the operating assumptions

    Single or multichannel; static or moving; offline or causal; simulated or real; target present or absent.

  3. 3

    Organize the method families

    Physical/statistical, discriminative, generative, and hybrid—then say what each predicts.

  4. 4

    Name representative architectures

    Choose two or three models from the catalog and explain why their structures match the task.

  5. 5

    Match metrics to claims

    Pair fidelity, perception, content/identity, task-specific behavior, and runtime cost.

  6. 6

    End with the boundary

    Name the hardest failure case and the evidence still needed before deployment.

“[Task] estimates [target] from [observation] using [side information]. Classical methods use [physical assumption]; discriminative models directly predict [mask/map/filter/waveform]; generative models use [prior or transport]; hybrids anchor [content/space/path] before refinement. I would evaluate [fidelity], [perception], [content/identity], and [task-specific behavior], then verify [latency/RTF]. The main unresolved risk is [failure mode] under [mismatch].”
